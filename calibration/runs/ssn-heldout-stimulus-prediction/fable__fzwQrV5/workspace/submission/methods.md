## Approach
We fit the recurrent weight matrix `W` by gradient descent *through the forward simulation* of the given SSN equations (forward Euler, `tau=0.5, k=0.5, n=2`, rates clipped at 0), implemented in PyTorch (`ssn.py`, `fit.py`). No finite differences of the noisy, coarsely sampled rates are ever taken: the loss compares the simulated trajectory, read out at the 61 observation times (t=0 excluded), with the observed rates. The observation model is additive Gaussian noise (sigma = 0.02, estimated from the 20% exact zeros at quiet times) clipped at zero, so the loss uses the *expected clipped* rate `mu*Phi(mu/sigma) + sigma*phi(mu/sigma)` rather than `mu`, which removes the upward bias of near-zero rates.

`W` is parametrised as `W_ij = sign_j * w1_ab * exp(-(d_ij^2 - 1)/(2 sigma_ab^2)) * exp(D_ij)` with zero diagonal: Dale's law by construction (column sign from the known E/I identity), a distance-dependent Gaussian kernel per E/I block (4 unit-distance amplitudes `w1_ab` and 4 widths `sigma_ab`, widths bounded in [0.5, 3.5] lattice units) and a per-connection log-normal deviation `D_ij` with ridge penalty `lambda * sum D_ij^2`. Optimiser: L-BFGS with strong-Wolfe line search, 150-200 iterations, training simulated at dt=0.02 (dt=0.01 for three of the members).

Stability is imposed, not hoped for: inside the loss the fitted network is also simulated on the *held-out drive* (which is known) and any rate above a cap (2.0, later 1.2, with the pulse amplitude inflated by 10%) is penalised quadratically. Members are then re-simulated at the full dt=0.01 resolution and discarded if non-finite or above 1.5 (the training recording never exceeds 0.94 under a 60% stronger drive).

The final `r_pred.npy` is the **mean of an ensemble** of stable members: full-data fits at lambda in {0.03, 0.1, 0.3} plus bagged fits (per-neuron exponential resampling weights on the loss, lambda 0.1 or 0.3). `ensemble.py` assembles it; `make_submission.py`/`run_*.sh`/`bag.py` produce the members.

## What the method targets
The only unknown shared between the two conditions is the recurrent connectivity `W`; constants, drive, and initial state are given. We estimate `W` under the structural priors that the task justifies (Dale's law, zero diagonal, spatially homogeneous distance-dependent kernels per E/I block) plus connection-specific deviations. The kernel parameters transfer to lattice regions the training stimulus never drove; the deviations are only learnable for the ~9 neurons activated by the training Gaussian and stay at the prior mean elsewhere. Because a supralinear network's response is a nonlinear function of `W`, and the kernel parameters turned out to be weakly identified from a single stimulus location, we predict with an ensemble mean (a posterior-predictive-style estimate) rather than a single point estimate of `W`.

## Validation performed
All numbers are nRMSE against the *noisy* training observations (noise floor roughly 0.32-0.47 depending on the window), so only differences matter.
- Leave-one-pulse-out (pulses 1, 3, 4 held out entirely, fit on the rest; `cv.py pulse`): feedforward (`W=0`) 0.60 / peak-region 0.42; structured-only 0.41 / 0.29; lambda=3: 0.42 / 0.31; lambda=0.3: 0.39 / 0.24; lambda=0.03: 0.36 / 0.14. Weaker regularisation predicts held-out pulses at the same location essentially at the noise floor.
- Leave-neurons-out (3 folds of 16 neurons whose observations are removed from the loss; `cv.py neuron`): two folds at the noise floor (0.33 vs floor 0.33; 1.07-1.10 vs floor 1.06), one fold (containing the single strongly amplified E neighbour of the stimulated site) worse than feedforward in the peak region (1.5 vs 0.49). `diag.py` showed this fold's fit flips the E->E kernel from strong/local to weak/broad, i.e. the kernel parameters are poorly identified, motivating the ensemble and the stability guard.
- Stability: every member is re-simulated on the held-out drive at dt=0.01; 4 of 19 fitted members diverged (at t~43, when the sweep reaches one E-rich region) despite a guard evaluated at dt=0.04, so the guard was moved to dt=0.02 with a 10% drive margin and cap 1.2. Diverged members are excluded; the final ensemble has 15 members (5 full-data fits, 10 bagged), all with eval peak rate 0.43-0.58. Members disagree with their mean by 0.15-0.60 nRMSE units of the ensemble's own std (see `ensemble_log.txt`), quantifying transfer uncertainty.
- `selfcheck.py` passes (shape, finiteness, non-negativity, max predicted rate 0.456).

## Budget used
About 2 h wall-clock total in a 4-core CPU sandbox (no GPU). One fit takes 1-3 min at dt=0.02 (about 6 min at dt=0.01); ~25 fits were run in total (CV folds, diagnostics, ensemble members), mostly 3-4 in parallel.

## Limitations
- The training stimulus drives one site, so connection-specific structure is learned only near it; elsewhere the prediction rests on the 8 kernel parameters, which are weakly identified (different neuron subsets give qualitatively different E->E kernels). The eval response in undriven regions is therefore uncertain; the ensemble mean hedges but will smooth genuine neuron-to-neuron heterogeneity.
- The fitted networks sit close to a runaway threshold; the stability cap and margin are priors, not measurements, and the chosen values (1.2, 10%) are judgment calls.
- Noise model assumed Gaussian-clipped with a fixed sigma; initial state assumed uniform 0.01 (t=0 column excluded from the fit). Fits used dt=0.02 for speed, predictions dt=0.01.
- Ridge strength was chosen by CV at the training location; it may not be optimal for spatial transfer.
